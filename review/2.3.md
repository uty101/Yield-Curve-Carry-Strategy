## What changed
- `src/curvecarry/svensson.py` — the λ form added to the module 1.3 created. `sv_loadings(tau, lam1, lam2)` (n × 4): the three NS loadings at λ1 plus the second curvature at λ2. `fit_fixed_lambdas` (OLS at a fixed pair). `lambda_pairs(grid)` — 351 ordered pairs with `λ2 > λ1 + step` from `config.nelson_siegel.ns_lambda_grid`. `fit(tau, y, grid)` — that grid, then `scipy.optimize.minimize(method="Nelder-Mead", xatol=1e-8, fatol=1e-14)` from the best pair, clipped to `[lo, hi]`, keeping the grid winner if the polish is worse and re-ordering the pair if the polish crosses it. `SVFit(beta0..beta3, lam1, lam2, lam_gap, rmse_bp, n_tenors)`. `fit_panel` → `data/processed/sv_params.parquet` (3,153 rows) and the `model = "sv"` rows appended to `ns_fitted.parquet` (now 70,776 rows).
- `bundesbank_to_lambda(row)` and `compare_bundesbank(cfg)` → `data/checks/svensson_vs_bundesbank.csv` (349 DE months).
- `src/curvecarry/cli.py` — `build --step svensson`. The step also rewrites `data/checks/fit_holdout.csv` with all three models (amendment 3): 361,491 rows.
- `tests/test_svensson.py` (8): the plan's five, plus amendment 2's `test_de_fit_for_a_row_outside_the_grid` and `test_bundesbank_to_lambda_swaps_labels_and_betas`, plus `test_fit_panel_and_holdout_shapes`.

**Deviation 1, and it is a correction to amendment 2 as written.** The amendment says to swap `(λ1, λ2)` and `(β2, β3)` where `1/τ2 < 1/τ1` "so both sides use λ2 > λ1 before comparing betas and lambdas". That swap is implemented exactly as written and `swapped` is recorded per month — **but it is a labelling convention, not an identity**. λ1 carries the *slope* loading `(1−e^{−λ1τ})/(λ1τ)` as well as the first curvature, so exchanging the two curvature terms leaves the curve unchanged only when β1 = 0. On the fixture row 1997-09-02 the relabelled parameters describe a curve 65.7 bp away from the original at 3 years. Consequences, both taken:
  - `rmse_bbk_bp` is computed from the **original, unswapped** parameters via `svensson_yield(τ, …, τ1, τ2)`. It is 0.000 bp at every one of the 349 months, which is the control the plan asks for. Had it been scored after the swap it would have been tens of bp and would have looked like a units or compounding error.
  - `beta_diff_max` is computed after the swap, as the amendment says. Where `swapped` is true it therefore compares a β1 fitted against λ1 with a β1 fitted against what is now labelled λ1 but was the Bundesbank's second decay. Read that column with the `swapped` flag beside it. Raised in the session review. Its median is 0.0323 but its mean is 2.4e8 — see the collapsed-pair finding below, which is the reason.
- `fit` does **not** enforce `λ2 > λ1 + step` during the Nelder-Mead polish — only `[lo, hi]`, which is what the plan says. The polish may therefore collapse the pair; it does so exactly (`lam_gap < 1e-12`) in 0.3% (GB) to 3.2% (JP) of months, where the design matrix has two identical columns and `lstsq` returns the minimum-norm split of β2 and β3. The fitted **curve** is unaffected; only the β2/β3 split is arbitrary. Not fixed here because constraining the polish is a choice the plan does not make (rule: never guess); reported below and in the session review.

## Not verified
- **Whether widening the grid would change any fitted curve.** The evidence above says the outside-grid λ are degenerate and that our in-grid fits reproduce the DE curves to 0.1 bp, so probably not by much — but that is an inference from Germany, the one country where the truth is published. What would settle it: refit every country on a wider grid and compare held-out RMSE month by month. Not done; it is the subject of the decision issue, and `ns_lambda_grid` is a config default that rule 14 forbids changing outside a step that says so.
- **Whether the 1e10 betas ever matter downstream.** Chart 3 (2.4) plots NS-free betas, not Svensson betas, and Phase 3 runs PCA on yields, not on these betas, so nothing in the plan reads them today. What would settle it: a `rcond` on `lstsq` large enough to return the minimum-norm solution, or a minimum separation on the polished pair. Both are choices the plan does not make; they are question 2 of the decision issue.
- **GB's held-out ratio of 4.66.** Svensson's in-sample RMSE on the UK curve is 0.6 bp — near-interpolation of 8 points with 6 parameters — so the ratio is large mostly because the denominator is small; its held-out RMSE of 2.8 bp is still the best of the three models. Not investigated further.

## Open
- The collapsed-pair beta blowup above. It is a numerical consequence of leaving the polish unconstrained, which is what the plan says to do. Question 2 of the decision issue.
- The amendment-2 swap is not curve-preserving (deviation 1). Implemented as instructed, with `rmse_bbk_bp` scored unswapped so the control still means something. Whether `beta_diff_max` should be computed before or after the swap is a question for the owner; it is in the session review.
