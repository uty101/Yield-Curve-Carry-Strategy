**The statistic changed in the fix round (2026-09-23).** `abs_cosine`, the
uncentred `|v_k^decade . v_k^full|`, is now the statistic PLAN.md 3.2 names,
and `abs_corr` is kept beside it as a secondary column. Pearson removes each
vector's mean, and for a level factor the mean is nearly the whole vector, so
`abs_corr` on PC1 measures the tilt of the level loading rather than whether
it is the same factor. `abs_corr` stays in the file because that tilt is a
real fact about a decade, not because it answers the stability question.

**Rules stated in full.** A decade is `<yyyy>-01 .. <yyyy+9>-12`, labelled
`1960s`…`2020s`. Each decade's changes are fitted on their own, with that
country's tenor set and the same sign rules, and each of PC1, PC2, PC3 is
compared with the full-sample vector. A decade with fewer than
`config.pca.stability_min_months` (24) change months is written with its
count and both statistics NaN — never dropped. The US is written **twice**:
`us_borderline_excluded = False` on all months and `True` without the
pre-`strategy_start` months whose 30-year zero-to-par gap exceeds
`config.pca.us_borderline_gap_bp` (50).

## Not verified

- **That the decade boundaries are the right cut.** They are calendar
  decades, which is what PLAN.md says, and they put the 1979–82 Volcker
  period across the 1970s/1980s line. A rolling 120-month window would
  settle whether the 1980s result is a decade fact or a boundary fact.
- **Whether JP's weak 1980s row is the 36-month count or the JGB curve.**
  Both are plausible; the row is just over the floor.
- **That the association in the second finding is more than six points.**
  Spearman 0.83 on six countries is one rank inversion away from perfect
  (GB's front end collapsed hardest but JP has the lowest PC2 cosine), and
  six is too few to put a standard error on. The US claim does not rest on
  it — the first and third findings are about the US alone.

## Open

- **Both statistics move on PC1 for every decade when the borderline months
  are excluded, including decades that lost no month** — US 2000s `abs_corr`
  goes 0.4015 → 0.3875 and `abs_cosine` 0.9718 → 0.9726.
  Tried: checking the decade-only fits, which are identical
  (`explained_share_decade` matches to 1e-12 and `n_months` is unchanged).
  The cause is that the *full-sample* vector both are measured against is
  refitted without the excluded months, so every comparison shifts a little.
  That is correct behaviour, but it means the two US blocks are not a
  like-for-like comparison row by row; the like-for-like comparison is the
  first finding above, which refits both 1980s vectors directly.
- **Whether the front-end variance collapse of the 2010s is itself caused by
  the zero lower bound, rather than merely coincident with it.** The
  collapse is measured (below) and it accounts for the loading shift; what
  is not shown is that the bound caused the collapse. The three countries
  whose front end compressed most are the three whose policy rate sat at or
  below zero longest, which is suggestive and nothing more on six countries.
  A test on the sub-periods within the decade, before and after each
  country's own liftoff, would settle it.
